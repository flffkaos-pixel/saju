Research Report #02

Historical Evolution of Saju:
From Ancient China to Modern Korea

Published: 2026-08-10 | Four Pillars Research Team

1. Origins and Development Timeline

  • Tang Dynasty (618–907 CE): Li Xuzhong formalized the Day Master concept, establishing the theoretical foundation of what would become Saju.
  • Song Dynasty (960–1279 CE): Xu Ziping refined the system, shifting emphasis from year pillar to day pillar as the "self." This became the standard used today.
  • Joseon Dynasty (1392–1897): Korean scholars adapted the Chinese system, developing unique interpretive frameworks like CheonGanYuBi and integrating Korean seasonal markers.
  • Modern Era (20th–21st Century): The tradition adapted to solar calendar usage while retaining the 60-sexagenary cycle calculation method.

2. Key Differentiators of Korean Saju

Korean Saju differs from Chinese BaZi in three key areas: (1) greater emphasis on the "Day Master vs. Month Branch" relationship, (2) integration of Korean seasonal terms (24 solar terms adapted to Korean latitude), and (3) a stronger cultural focus on interpersonal compatibility (Gunghap).

3. Modern Interpretation Challenges

The transition from agricultural to information society raises questions about which aspects of the tradition remain relevant. The descriptive (not predictive) nature of the system allows for reinterpretation without losing cultural authenticity.

4. Conclusion

The Four Pillars tradition is not a static system but a living framework that has adapted across cultures and centuries. Understanding its historical evolution helps practitioners distinguish between universal mathematical principles and culturally specific interpretations.
